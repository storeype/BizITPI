How the model works

Door-to-door KPIs

Annual cost covers the truck leg to the terminal, main leg, extra handling, truck waiting, the 2026 Dutch and Flemish truck charges, the Liefkenshoek toll and barge port dues. Time is hours per container trip; CO2 is well-to-wheel. Time is also priced into cost: capital tied up in transit, safety stock for unreliable lead times, missed vessel cut-offs and low-water barge delay.

One weighted score

Each KPI is rescaled per corridor: best mode 100, worst 0. The scores are weighted by the sliders. With only two modes, as on Antwerp, equal cost and time weights with no CO2 weight produce an exact tie. Each mode is flagged against a CO2 red line from PVM’s SBTi scope 3 target.

Public, traceable data

77 inputs from 39 sources: KiM/Panteia cost figures, CO2emissiefactoren.nl, Staatscourant, Viapass, and the OSRM, EuRIS and OpenRailRouting route planners. Volume and port split are proxies until PVM data replaces them.

Scope check: why we stayed at two lanes

We tried to widen the analysis from export containers on two port lanes to every PVM lane, with real customer destinations, on the expectation that AI could assemble the data. The public data could not support it, so the analysis stays at the two lanes it can answer honestly.

What we tried

Total lane visibility: 100% of PVM’s outbound goods by destination, with share of cargo, TEU and weight per lane, including maritime and multimodal legs.

What we found

The only public volume proxy (Dutch confectionery exports) is 5.6× Breda’s output and leaves out domestic sales. No public source names PVM’s ship-to locations, and PVM runs 31+ plants. Country-level lanes would be too aggregated to support a decision.

What would unlock it

PVM’s own shipment data (destinations, volumes by lane and mode) would let the same model run lane by lane. Until then, volume and port split remain proxies.
